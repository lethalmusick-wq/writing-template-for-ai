# UNLETTERED — Premise (Hero's Journey)

Status: pre-Stage 1 idea. Not canon until the author folds it into `START_HERE.md` and approves a blueprint.

## Logline

A tallow-renderer's son with an illegal gift for magic flees the kingdom that would chain his power to a noble master. He spends six years learning the dead language of spellcraft in the places it was first written. When he returns as the most dangerous wizard alive, he finds that the war, the purges, and the people he loved were all shaped by what he did while he was gone.

## The magic (why his education has to happen on the road)

Magic is a grammar. The vanished Vauri carved it into their cities, roads, and tombs, and a spell is a sentence in their language. In the kingdom of Asterhold, the Lantern Collegium teaches nobles a frozen liturgy of about four hundred memorized phrases. Lowborn are forbidden to learn the script at all. Any commoner caught casting gets a **tithe-mark**: a Vauri clause cut into the skin that makes their power belong to a named noble mage, who draws on it like a well.

You can't learn the living language from a book. It survives only where it was written, so Edrik has to go to those places. Each stretch of the journey teaches him a different part of its grammar:

| Region | What it teaches | Physical cost |
|---|---|---|
| The Thornmarch (wild borderland) | **Nouns**: naming a thing truly so it obeys | Starvation, bog fever, bog-wights |
| Saelim steppe (rival horse-clans) | **Verbs**, sung by many voices together | A year of indentured labor |
| Kessary (rival merchant republic) | **Conditionals**: contracts, if/then magic | Prison, a mercenary ambush |
| Vael-Orun (the forgotten city) | **The first person**: how a speaker binds himself | Nearly everything |

## The journey

**1. Ordinary World.** Edrik Hask, seventeen, works the rendering vats in Lowmere, Asterhold's river slum. He sells small illegal charms to dockworkers: knots that keep rope from fraying, a word that brings a fever down. He supports his mother Wenna and his eleven-year-old sister Nell. His closest friend, Aldo Pell, is a clerk's son studying for the Collegium entrance exam. Aldo is the only person who knows what Edrik can do.

**2. Call to Adventure.** A salvage barge brings up a Vauri boundary stone. Edrik touches it and reads it without meaning to, and the stone burns blue through half the dock. Magister Oriel Sand, the Collegium's inquisitor for the river wards, comes looking for whoever did it.

**3. Refusal.** Edrik keeps his head down and lets a drunk dockhand take the blame. That works until Aldo names him in exchange for a scholarship seat. (First misjudgment: Edrik never imagined Aldo would do it.)

**4. Meeting the Mentor.** Ysolde Marrow is a disgraced former Collegium wardkeeper. Her tithe-mark was cut so badly it left her half-crippled. She gets Edrik out of the city through the tallow sewers. She is rude and selfish and doesn't teach him spells. She teaches him to *read*: how to find the grammar in a ruined wall, and how to tell a live inscription from a dead one. She also has her own reason for going. She wants to reach Vael-Orun and cut the mark out of her body.

**5. Crossing the Threshold.** The Thornmarch is a hundred miles of drowned forest where Vauri waystones still half-work and the animals have learned to use them. Edrik learns nouns by surviving: he names a bog-wight correctly and it lets him pass, and he gets the name of a flooded ford wrong and nearly drowns.

**6. Tests, Allies, Enemies.**

- **First lost fight.** The Collegium hires the Iron Tally, a free company led by Captain Dagny Rook, to bring him back. They catch the pair at a ford. Edrik casts the biggest thing he knows, and Rook walks through it and breaks three fingers of his casting hand with a mace haft. Ysolde surrenders herself so he can run. He crosses into the steppe alone, unable to gesture, so he has to learn to cast by voice.
- **A mistake with a price.** Feverish and desperate in Saelim country, he speaks a word over a spring to make it give water. It goes foul, and a sacred herd dies. The Saelim don't kill him. They indenture him for one year to a clan singer named Teodh, who despises him. Saelim magic is choral: a verb carries only when many voices sing it. They think a lone wizard is a thief who takes from everyone around him. Edrik spends a winter learning they are partly right. Teodh becomes his friend, slowly and grudgingly.
- **A misjudged ally.** Freed, he and Teodh go to Kessary, a merchant republic that has fought Asterhold over river tolls for a century. Factor Ammon Quill, a charming spymaster, offers passage to Vael-Orun in exchange for what Edrik knows of Vauri conditionals. Edrik takes the deal because it looks fair. Quill builds siege-contracts out of that grammar, and Kessary uses them two years later to break Asterhold's river forts. Edrik won't learn this until he is home.
- **Outmatched again.** Quill has quietly re-hired the Iron Tally to make sure Edrik never brings his knowledge to anyone else. Rook corners them in the Kessary salt flats. Edrik doesn't win. He and Teodh survive by collapsing a Vauri cistern and hiding in it for three days.

**7. Approach to the Inmost Cave.** Vael-Orun is a Vauri city whose grammar was never switched off. The streets rearrange according to sentences still being spoken by something underneath. Moths made of glass feed on memory. Edrik loses whole days, and then the sound of his father's voice, and he never gets it back.

**8. The Ordeal.** At the city's heart is the Archivist, a thing made of living syntax that rewrites anyone who speaks near it. Edrik is completely outmatched. Teodh sings a Saelim verb to cover his escape and is rewritten mid-song into an inscription on the wall. Edrik survives by doing the one thing the Collegium never teaches: he reads the Archivist's grammar as it is spoken, and finds the first-person clause it uses to bind itself.

**9. The Reward.** That clause is the source text of the tithe-mark. The Collegium's mark is a crude, corrupted copy of a Vauri oath of self-binding, and anyone who reads the original can read the copy backward. Edrik can unmake tithe-marks. He can also put them on other people. He has become the most fluent speaker of Vauri in five hundred years, at the cost of a friend, two fingers that never set right, and part of his own memory.

**10. The Road Back.** Rook catches him a third time on the way out. This time he beats her and her company outright, without killing anyone. He reads her life-debt to Quill off her contract and breaks it. She follows him home, partly as a prisoner and partly because she wants to see what he does next.

**11. Resurrection.** At Asterhold's border he learns the cost of his deal with Quill. Asterhold is at war, the river forts are gone, and his grammar did it. The final test is a choice, not a fight (see below).

**12. Return with the Elixir.** He brings back the ability to free every bound mage in the kingdom. Whether he uses it, how, and what it costs is the climax of the book.

## The return: six years later

**The kingdom.** The old king is dead and the queen is nine years old. Her regent depends on the Collegium for every battle-mage on the Kessary front. Because of the war, tithing has spread from "caught casting" to *every* lowborn child tested at age ten. The Collegium now governs in all but name. Lowmere burned in a riot four years ago and was rebuilt as barracks.

**His mother, Wenna.** The Collegium marked her for harboring him. She isn't gifted, so the mark holds almost nothing, but she has carried it for six years as a public sign of his crime. She is relieved he is alive and furious he came back so late. She won't let him remove the mark until he tells her what he plans to do with everyone else's.

**His sister, Nell (now seventeen, the age he was when he left).** She tested gifted at ten and was marked and bound to a minor lord. She has also become a leader in the Unlit, an underground of illegal casters who turned "the Hask boy who escaped" into a folk hero. The real Edrik is quieter, more cautious, and more compromised than the legend, and Nell can't forgive him for it. She wants every mark broken tonight, whatever happens at the front.

**Aldo Pell (the friend who sold him).** He is now a Magister, and the leading reformer inside the Collegium. He has spent six years pushing to soften the tithe law one clause at a time. He is sincerely sorry, and sincerely afraid. If Edrik breaks every mark at once, Aldo's careful reform becomes pointless and the front collapses. He asks Edrik to work with him slowly, and he is the hardest person in the book to say no to.

**Oriel Sand (the inquisitor).** Now Archmagister. He offers Edrik a title, a seat on the Collegium, and his family's marks quietly removed. In exchange, Edrik would teach the Collegium what he learned and leave the system standing.

**Ysolde Marrow (the mentor).** She is alive in a Collegium cell, sixty pounds lighter. They kept her as bait in case he came back. She is the first mark he breaks, which tells the Collegium exactly what he can do.

**Dagny Rook (the enemy).** She is now the one person who has seen what he can do in a fight. Both the regent and Quill's agents try to buy what she knows.

**Kessary.** Quill sends an envoy. He doesn't threaten. He offers thanks, publicly, in front of the court. That one act makes Edrik a traitor in the eyes of everyone who used to trust him.

**The final dilemma.** If he breaks every tithe-mark at once, thousands go free, the Collegium falls overnight, and Asterhold loses the war, with Lowmere first in line for Kessary's siege-contracts. If he works within the system, he becomes the new Oriel Sand. The ending has him find a third path: he uses what the Saelim taught him (power that only works when many speak together) together with what Vael-Orun taught him (a self-binding oath), and pays for it personally.
